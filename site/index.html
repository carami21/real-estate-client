What it does

Six jobs that currently live in your inbox.

PUTSOMESPECONIT is one desk for a residential practice: the listings waiting on a decision, the deals that have gone quiet, and the money owed at the end of the month. It is built for a team of two to forty, not for an enterprise rollout.

A listing cannot go live broken

Every listing runs a checklist before it publishes: photo count, disclosures attached, square footage matched against the county record, a seller signature on page three. When something fails, it names the check by name instead of saying "incomplete".

  • Only 6 photos — 8 required
  • Square footage conflicts with county record
  • Missing lead-based paint disclosure

The three above are real checks from the demo queue.

Chase a stale listing before it goes cold

The queue sorts worst-first by days sitting, so the fourteen-day-old townhouse with four photos and a blank lease term is the first thing you see, not something you find in a quarterly review. Average days on market is on the same screen, next to last quarter's.

Commission maths you can hand to an agent

Each agent's split is stored once, so a payout run shows gross, split and net per person without a spreadsheet rebuild. A payout can be held with a reason attached rather than quietly skipped.

Showings counted, not guessed at

Showings booked this week roll up per listing and per agent, so you can tell a seller their house was shown eleven times and point at the dates. Quiet listings sort straight to the top.

Documents live on the listing

Purchase contract, disclosures and floor plan hang off the listing record, not off whoever last emailed them. Open a property and the paperwork is the second tab.

One roster, four offices

Listing agent, buyer's agent, transaction coordinator and compliance reviewer each see the same record with their own queue on top of it. Share of active inventory by office is a chart, not a phone call.

A day on PUTSOMESPECONIT

Thursday, from the first coffee to the last payout.

Nothing below is a notification you have to go looking for. The four moments are the four times in a normal day that the software actually changed what happened next.

  1. 7:40am

    Nine listings are already sorted

    The review queue is ordered by what is most likely to cost you the deal: photo counts under eight, disclosures missing, anything sitting longer than a week. You work down it instead of opening twelve tabs to find out where you stand.

  2. 11:15am

    A price check fires on the Tempe ranch

    The list price came in eighteen per cent under comparable sales. PUTSOMESPECONIT flagged it with the comp set attached, so the conversation with the seller happened this morning — not after an appraisal came back and the whole thing had to be re-papered.

  3. 2:30pm

    Escrow opens and the file assembles itself

    The coordinator marks the Gilbert sale open. Contract, disclosures and the signed addendum are already on the listing, so the escrow packet is a download rather than an afternoon of forwarding attachments.

  4. 6:05pm

    October payouts, one screen

    Gross, split and net per agent, with one payout held because a commission demand has not come back from title. The hold carries its reason, so nobody has to ask twice why their number is different this month.

Questions

The five things people ask first.

We have four years of deals in spreadsheets. What happens to them?

They get imported, column by column, with you in the room. Listings, agents, splits and closed volume map onto its own records; anything that does not map cleanly stays in a notes field rather than being silently dropped. Expect to spend an afternoon on it, not a quarter — and keep the spreadsheets afterwards, because nothing here deletes your originals.

Does it talk to the MLS, or to my IDX feed?

Not yet, and that is worth being straight about. PUTSOMESPECONIT is the desk you work from before a listing is fit to publish — the checklist, the documents, the money. MLS and IDX connections are per-board, contractual, and different in every market, so they are a build for your board rather than a switch to flip. Ask in the form below and you will get an honest answer about your board, not a roadmap promise.

My agents live in Excel. Do they have to stop?

No. Every table on the desk exports, so an agent who wants to model their own split in Excel still can. What changes is which copy is the real one: the brokerage's numbers stop living in whichever file was emailed last, and start living in one record everybody reads from.

What does it cost?

There is no price list on this page, because there is no price list yet. PUTSOMESPECONIT was built for one brokerage and is being fitted to others one at a time, and what it costs depends on how many agents you have and how much of your old data needs moving. Tell Noah the size of your team in the form below and he will quote the work rather than publish a number he would have to walk back.
